The 4am problem

Ideas don’t wait. At best they resurface.

Voice memos will record your idea, but you can’t build on a memo. A DAW will build it, but by the time the session’s open, the spark has moved on.

Hook is the missing middle — quick as a memo, and providing enough tools to write a song.

Loop & Layers

Play it through once and it starts looping.

Put on your headphones and pick out your tempo and time signature: 4/4, 3/4, 6/8, 5/8, 7/8. Hook counts you in, you put the idea down, and the loop is going right when you play, sing, or say your part.

Not one for counting bars? Go Endless — just record until you’re done. Trade the bar counter for a plain clock if you like. The musical stuff is all still there; it just steps out of the way.

  1. 01

    Set the feel

    Tempo, time signature, bars — or Endless, when you’d rather just hit record and go — and a “hear this tempo” button so you can trust it before you commit.

  2. 02

    Get counted in

    Four beats, an accent on one, and you’re rolling. The moment you finish, it loops.

  3. 03

    Stack your layers

    Layer up to 6 layers per stack on headphones, each with its own waveform, volume, mute, and name. Group the stacks. Keep writing new parts.

Words, lyrics & chords

Write your words in rainbows.

As you type, rhymes find each other, matched by sound, not spelling, so the slant rhymes count too. Each rhyme family takes a color, and you can see the shape of a verse the way you’d hear it. And when you want them, chords go right over the words — letter or Roman numeral, whichever way you prefer. Export your song to take to any gig or keep for your own records.

The part we needed to solve

Your layers loop in time. On any headphones.

Here’s the quiet problem with recording on a phone mic with Bluetooth headphones: latency, something any recording engineer fears. Most apps ignore it, and your looped layers drift late. Hook listens instead. The first time you record with a new pair of Bluetooth headphones, it plays a small sound and listens for it to come back. It remembers each pair forever. It doesn’t guess, it’s smarter than that.

Good questions

A few honest answers.

Will it work with my headphones — even Bluetooth?

Any headphones, wired or Bluetooth. The first time you use a new pair, Hook plays a quick sound and listens for it to come back — so it learns exactly how much delay to cancel. After that, every layer lands right where you sang it, and it remembers each pair for next time. Nothing to set, nothing to tune.

Why can’t I hear my own vocals in my headphones?

Just like a vocal booth: once the takes start piling up and it gets hard to hear yourself, slip your headphones off one ear and keep layering. Nothing’s wrong — one ear on the mix, one on the room is how singers have tracked forever.

Does Hook replace Ableton, or any DAW?

No — it’s the step before. Hook is for catching and shaping the song while it’s still alive; when it’s ready for full production, export your masters and stems and take them into Ableton — or any DAW. Hook gets the song down; your DAW turns it into a record.
