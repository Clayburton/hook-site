The 4am problem

The idea doesn’t wait.

Nobody’s best idea shows up with a hot mic and a room waiting on them. It lands in the car, in line at the store, at 4am — already humming, already half gone.

Voice Memos will hold it, but you can’t build on a memo. A DAW will build it, but by the time the session’s open, the spark has moved on. Hook is the missing middle — quick as a memo, and it becomes a song.

The loop

Play it once.
It’s already looping.

Slide to a tempo, tap one out, or listen until it feels right. Pick your time — 4/4, 3/4, 6/8, 5/8, 7/8. Hook counts you in, you put the idea down, and the loop is going before your hands are off the strings. Or the keys. Or the desk you were drumming on.

    Set the feel

    Tempo, time signature, bars — and a “hear this tempo” button so you can trust it before you commit.

  2. 2

    Get counted in

    Four beats, an accent on one, and you’re rolling. The moment you finish, it loops.

  3. 3

    Stack your layers

    Layer after layer on headphones, each with its own waveform, volume, mute, and name. Group them. Keep going.

Lyrics & chords

Write the words while they’re warm.

A real writing surface in a warm serif, right under the loop. As you type, rhymes find each other — matched by sound, not spelling, so the slant rhymes count too. Each rhyme family takes a color, and you can see the shape of a verse the way you’d hear it. Touch a word, hang a chord on it.

This box is live — add a line, watch the rhymes find each other.

The part nobody else nails

Your layers land where you put them. On any headphones.

Here’s the quiet problem with recording on a phone: wireless headphones hold the sound back a little, and every pair is different. Most apps ignore it, and your layers drift late. The rest bury a latency slider in settings and hand the problem to you.

Hook listens instead. The first time you record with a new pair, it plays a small sound and listens for it to come back — three seconds, once. After that, every layer you stack lands exactly where you played it. It remembers each pair forever. A new pair just asks once.

And when it can’t get a clean read — a loud room, a bad moment — it says so and tries again. It doesn’t guess.
